\uline{2) Useful notions}

$\bullet$ \uline{Event}: A physical occurrence in space and time \hfill (associated to a specific S-T point)

\textit{Remark}: An event has no spatial extension and no duration.

\textit{Ex}: Emission of a photon

\hspace{1.5em}$-$ The presence of a point-like particle at some S-T point.

$\bullet$ \uline{Observers}: Essentially, a reference frame

\textit{Note}: we can switch reference frames through coordinate transformations

$\bullet$ \uline{Local time}: Each observer has a notion of time and all observers measure time in the same way

$\bullet$ \uline{Worldline}: The path that a point-like object follows in Space and Time

\textit{Note}: For extended objects: world sheets and world volumes

\begin{align*}
\text{-- From Newtonian gravity}\quad&\longrightarrow\quad \vec F=-M_g\,\vec\nabla\phi,
&M_g &: \text{``gravitational mass''}\\
\text{-- From Newtonian mech}\quad&\longrightarrow\quad \vec F=M_i\,\vec a,
&M_i &: \text{``inertial mass''}
\end{align*}

\begin{center}
$M_i\stackrel{?}{=}M_g$\\[-0.2em]
yes, because we observe so
\end{center}

Galilean equivalence principle: \qquad $M_i=M_g$

\[
  \Longrightarrow\quad \vec a=-\vec\nabla\phi
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \text{universality of free fall}
\]

$\bullet$ \uline{Case 0}: Elevator in outer space, No forces $\Longrightarrow P$ just floats

$\bullet$ \uline{Case 1}: Still in outer space, elevator accelerated, w/ const ace $\vec g$

\hspace{1.5em}$\Longrightarrow P$ approaches the floor

$\bullet$ \uline{Case 2}: Elevator brought in const. gravitational field

\hspace{1.5em}$\Longrightarrow P$ approaches the floor

$\longrightarrow$ Both $R$ and $R'$ agree on these statements

\textbf{But} $R'$ cannot distinguish 1 from 2.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\color{noteGreen}\large Gravity = Acceleration}
\end{center}

\[
  L(t)=\frac{1}{2}gt^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ddot L(t)=g
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad z'=z-\frac{1}{2}gt^2
\]
\[
  t'=t\qquad(\text{ignoring SR})
\]

$-$ In $R$: \qquad $\vec F=M\,\ddot{\vec z}$

$-$ In $R'$: \qquad $\vec F'=M\,\ddot{\vec z}'=M\,\ddot{\vec z}-M\vec g\Longrightarrow \vec F'=\vec F-M\vec g$

$\Longrightarrow$ the acceleration of $R'$ wrt $R$ looks exactly like gravity in $R'$.

$\bullet$ \uline{Gravitational redshift}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth,scale=0.72]

  \draw[thick,decorate,decoration={snake,amplitude=2pt,segment length=5pt}] (1.2,1.05) -- (1.2,3.45) node[midway,right] {$\gamma$};
  \draw[thick,decorate,decoration={snake,amplitude=2pt,segment length=5pt}] (3.9,1.05) -- (3.9,3.45) node[midway,right] {$\gamma$};
  \draw[<->] (0.35,1.0) -- (0.35,3.5) node[midway,left] {$d$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

$P$: emitter \qquad $Q$: Receiver

$-$ photon of frequency $f_P$ emitted from $P$

$Q$: frequency measured by $Q$ ($f_Q$)?

$S_Q/c^2$: Photon ToF $\Delta t=d/c$

During the same time the velocity of $PQ$ increases by $\Delta v=g\Delta t=\dfrac{gd}{c}$.

In the acc. R.F. $Q$ will see a doppler-shifted frequency $f_Q$

\[
  \frac{f_Q}{f_P}=1-\frac{\Delta v}{c}=1-\frac{gd}{c^2}
\]

\begin{center}
\large Equivalence Principle $\ :\ $ Acceleration $\ \Longleftrightarrow\ $ gravity
\end{center}

$\Longrightarrow$ In the presence of a gravitational field, and in a fixed RF, photons will be redshifted as
\[
  \frac{f_Q}{f_P}=1-\frac{gd}{c^2}
\]

\vspace{1em}
\textit{Equivalently}: clocks at $Q$ tick slower than clocks at $P$.
