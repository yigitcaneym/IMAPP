\section{The group $S_3$}
The six permutations of three objects are
\[
S_3=\{e,(12),(13),(23),(123),(132)\}.
\]
The multiplication table is obtained by composing permutations. Since $(12)(23)=(123)$ whereas $(23)(12)=(132)$, the group is non-Abelian.

\section{The dihedral group $D_3$}
$D_3$ is the symmetry group of an equilateral triangle. It contains three rotations and three reflections:
\[
D_3=\{e,r,r^2,s_1,s_2,s_3\},\qquad r^3=e,
\]
with presentation
\[
D_3=\langle r,s\mid r^3=e,\ s^2=e,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
\]
The rotations form a subgroup isomorphic to $C_3$. The group is generated by one rotation and one reflection, so $\operatorname{rank}(D_3)=2$. In fact, $D_3\cong S_3$.

\section{Generators}
A subset $S\subseteq G$ is a generating set if every group element can be written as a finite product of elements of $S$ and their inverses. We write
\[
G=\langle S\rangle.
\]
For $C_3=\{e,g,g^2\}$, one generator suffices: $C_3=\langle g\rangle$ and its rank is one.

\section{Homomorphisms}
A homomorphism $f:G\to H$ preserves the composition law:
\[
f(g_1g_2)=f(g_1)f(g_2).
\]
As an example, map $D_3$ to $C_2=\{e,c\}$ by sending rotations to $e$ and reflections to $c$:
\[
f(e)=f(r)=f(r^2)=e,\qquad f(s_i)=c.
\]
The relation $s_i s_j=r^k$ ensures that the product rule is respected.

\section{Kernel and isomorphism}
The kernel measures which part of $G$ collapses to the identity:
\[
\Ker(f)=\{g\in G\mid f(g)=e_H\}.
\]
For the map above, $\Ker(f)=\{e,r,r^2\}$.

An isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism. Isomorphic finite groups have identical multiplication structure even when their elements are represented differently. For example,
\[
C_4\cong\{1,\mathrm{i},-1,-\mathrm{i}\}\cong \{\text{rotations by }0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}.
\]

\section{Endomorphisms and automorphisms}
An endomorphism is a homomorphism from a group to itself. An automorphism is a bijective endomorphism. The automorphisms of $G$ form a group,
\[
\Aut(G)=\{f:G\to G\mid f\text{ is bijective and }f(g_1g_2)=f(g_1)f(g_2)\}.
\]
For $(\Z,+)$, the only automorphisms are $n\mapsto n$ and $n\mapsto -n$, so $\Aut(\Z)\cong C_2$.

\section{Cayley's theorem}
Every finite group of order $n$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of the symmetric group $S_n$. Thus every finite abstract group can be represented as a permutation group.

\section*{Summary}
$S_3$ and $D_3$ provide the central examples of small non-Abelian groups. Homomorphisms preserve structure, their kernels identify collapsed subgroups, and Cayley's theorem embeds every finite group into a permutation group.
