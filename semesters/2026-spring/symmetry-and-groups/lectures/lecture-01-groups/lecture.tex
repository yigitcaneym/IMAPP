\section{Groups}
A group $G$ is a set equipped with an internal composition law, written multiplicatively, such that the following axioms hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*.]
  \item \textbf{Identity:} there exists $e\in G$ such that $eg=ge=g$ for every $g\in G$.
  \item \textbf{Inverse:} for every $g\in G$ there is $g^{-1}\in G$ with $gg^{-1}=g^{-1}g=e$.
  \item \textbf{Associativity:} $(g_1g_2)g_3=g_1(g_2g_3)$ for all $g_1,g_2,g_3\in G$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Types of groups}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{finite group} has finitely many elements. Its order is $|G|$; for example, the permutation group $S_n$ has $|S_n|=n!$.
  \item A \textbf{discrete infinite group} has infinitely many but countably many elements, such as $(\Z,+)$.
  \item A \textbf{continuous group} has elements parametrized by one or more continuous parameters. The number of independent parameters is the dimension of the group; rotations provide a standard example.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related definitions}
A semigroup has a closed associative composition law but need not contain an identity or inverses. For instance, $(\N^*,+)$ is a semigroup.

The commutator of two elements is
\[
[g_1,g_2]=g_1g_2g_1^{-1}g_2^{-1}.
\]
The elements commute when $g_1g_2=g_2g_1$. A group is \textbf{Abelian} precisely when all its elements commute; $(\Z,+)$ is Abelian.

For a finite group, the order of an element $g$ is the smallest positive integer $n$ satisfying
\[
\Ord(g)=n,\qquad g^n=e.
\]

\section{Cyclic groups}
A cyclic group is generated by the powers of a single element:
\[
C_n=\langle g\rangle=\{e,g,g^2,\ldots,g^{n-1}\},\qquad g^n=e.
\]
Every cyclic group is Abelian. Geometrically, $C_n$ can be realized as the rotations about a fixed point through angles $2\pi k/n$.

\section{Multiplication tables}
For a finite group, the multiplication table records the complete group structure. The rearrangement property says that multiplying every element on the left or right by a fixed $g_0\in G$ permutes the elements of $G$. Consequently, every element appears exactly once in every row and every column.

\section{The symmetric group}
$S_n$ is the group of all permutations of an $n$-element set. A permutation is a bijection of the set to itself, and the group operation is composition. For example, in $S_3$,
\[
(12)(23)=(123),\qquad (23)(12)=(132),
\]
so the order of composition matters: $S_3$ is non-Abelian.

\section*{Summary}
Groups encode reversible composition laws. The first useful examples are cyclic groups and permutation groups; multiplication tables and element orders make their structure explicit.
