\section{Natural units}
Particle physics commonly uses $\hbar=c=1$. The conversion factors are
\[
\hbar=1.055\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s},\qquad c=2.998\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},
\qquad 1\,\mathrm{GeV}=1.602\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J}.
\]
In natural units, time has dimensions of inverse energy and length has dimensions of inverse energy:
\[
[T]=E^{-1},\qquad [L]=E^{-1}.
\]
Useful conversions are $1\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}\simeq0.197\,\mathrm{fm}$ and $1\,\mathrm{fm}\simeq5.07\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$.

\section{Mandelstam variables}
For a $2\to2$ process with momenta $p_1,p_2\to p_3,p_4$,
\[
s=(p_1+p_2)^2,\qquad t=(p_1-p_3)^2,\qquad u=(p_1-p_4)^2.
\]
For fixed external masses, $s+t+u=\sum_i m_i^2$. In the centre-of-mass frame, $\sqrt{s}$ is the total energy.

\section{Decay and the Breit-Wigner form}
The population of unstable particles follows
\[
N(t)=N(0)e^{-\Gamma t},\qquad \tau=\Gamma^{-1},
\]
where $\Gamma$ is the total decay width. A finite lifetime gives an energy distribution around the resonance energy $E_0$:
\[
\rho(E)\propto\frac{\Gamma/2}{(E-E_0)^2+(\Gamma/2)^2}.
\]
The full width at half maximum is $\Gamma$.

For a resonance $R$ produced and then decaying, $A+B\to R\to A+B$, the cross-section is enhanced near $\sqrt{s}=M_R$. In the narrow-width approximation, $\Gamma\ll M_R$, the resonant factor approaches a delta function and production and decay can be approximately factorized:
\[
\sigma(A B\to R\to X)\simeq\sigma(A B\to R)\,\mathrm{Br}(R\to X).
\]

\section*{Summary}
Natural units simplify relativistic calculations, Mandelstam variables organize $2\to2$ kinematics, and unstable particles appear as Breit-Wigner resonances with width set by their lifetime.
