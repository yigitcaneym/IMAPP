\section{Fields and the action}
A classical field assigns a quantity $\phi(x)$ to every point of a $D$-dimensional spacetime. The dynamics are encoded in a local Lagrangian density,
\[
\mathcal L=\mathcal L(\phi,\partial_\mu\phi;x),
\]
and the action is
\[
S[\phi]=\int_{\Omega}\dd^D x\,\mathcal L(\phi,\partial_\mu\phi;x).
\]
Locality means that the density depends on the field and its derivatives at one spacetime point.

\section{Euler-Lagrange equations}
Vary the field, $\phi\to\phi+\delta\phi$, and require the action to be stationary. To first order,
\[
\delta S=\int\dd^D x\left(
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}\delta\phi+
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\partial_\mu\delta\phi
\right).
\]
Integrating the second term by parts gives a bulk term and a boundary term:
\[
\delta S=\int\dd^D x\left[
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}-
\partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right)
\right]\delta\phi+\text{boundary term}.
\]
For variations that vanish on the boundary, the Euler-Lagrange equation is
\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}-
\partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right)=0.}
\]

\section{Functional derivatives}
A functional maps a function to a number, $F:\{\phi\}\to\R$. Its functional derivative is defined by
\[
\frac{\delta F[\phi]}{\delta\phi(x)}
=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\frac{F[\phi+\varepsilon\delta_x]-F[\phi]}{\varepsilon},
\]
where the Dirac delta plays the role of a unit variation. In particular,
\[
\frac{\delta\phi(y)}{\delta\phi(x)}=\delta^{(D)}(x-y).
\]
For a quadratic functional,
\[
F[\phi]=\int\dd^D x\,\dd^D y\,\phi(x)G(x,y)\phi(y),
\]
the derivative receives contributions from both occurrences of $\phi$.

\section{Hamiltonian formalism}
For a discrete coordinate $q_i$, the conjugate momentum is $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$ and $H=\sum_i p_i\dot q_i-L$. In the continuum, define the momentum density
\[
\pi(x)=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_t\phi(x))},
\]
and the Hamiltonian density
\[
\mathcal H=\pi\dot\phi-\mathcal L,qquad H=\int\dd^{D-1}x\,\mathcal H.
\]
The Hamiltonian singles out time, whereas the Lagrangian formalism treats time and space in a more symmetric way.

\section{Symmetries}
Transformations may be continuous or discrete. Continuous transformations can be infinitesimal and may act on coordinates, fields, or both. A transformation is a symmetry when it leaves the action invariant:
\[
S[\phi']=S[\phi].
\]
The invariance can be exact or can differ by a total divergence. Under the usual boundary assumptions, a total divergence contributes only a boundary term and does not change the equations of motion. This observation leads to Noether's theorem: every continuous differentiable symmetry is associated with a conserved current.

\section*{Summary}
The action principle supplies the field equations, functional derivatives generalize ordinary differentiation to field space, and the Hamiltonian is obtained by a Legendre transform. Continuous action symmetries are the starting point for conserved quantities.
