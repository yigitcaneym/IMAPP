\section{Pictures of time evolution}
With a time-independent Hamiltonian, the evolution operator is
\[
U(t,t_0)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\ii}{\hbar}H(t-t_0)\right].
\]
In the Schrödinger picture states evolve and operators are fixed. In the Heisenberg picture,
\[
|\psi\rangle_H=U^\dagger|\psi(t)\rangle_S,\qquad A_H(t)=U^\dagger A_S U,
\]
so the state is time independent and operators carry the time dependence.

For perturbation theory, split the Hamiltonian into a solvable part and an interaction,
\[
H=H_0+H_{\mathrm{int}}.
\]
The interaction picture evolves states with $H_{\mathrm{int}}$ and operators with $H_0$:
\[
|\psi(t)\rangle_I=U_0^\dagger(t,t_0)|\psi(t)\rangle_S,
\qquad A_I(t)=U_0^\dagger A_S U_0.
\]
The operator equation is
\[
\ii\hbar\frac{\dd A_I}{\dd t}=[A_I,H_0]+\ii\hbar\left(\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}\right)_I.
\]

\section{The quantum harmonic oscillator}
In dimensionless variables, $[X,P]=\ii$, and the ladder operators are
\[
a=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(X+\ii P),\qquad a^\dagger=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(X-\ii P),
\qquad [a,a^\dagger]=1.
\]
The number operator $N=a^\dagger a$ satisfies
\[
[N,a]=-a,\qquad [N,a^\dagger]=a^\dagger.
\]
Its eigenstates obey
\[
N|n\rangle=n|n\rangle,
\qquad a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle,
\qquad a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle.
\]
The state $|0\rangle$ is the vacuum, defined by $a|0\rangle=0$. The ladder operators remove or add one quantum of excitation.

\section{From a discrete string to a field}
A discrete chain of masses and springs has a finite set of coordinates $q_i(t)$. Taking the continuum limit sends the lattice spacing $a\to0$ while keeping the mass density and elastic modulus fixed. The discrete variables become a displacement field
\[
q_i(t)\longrightarrow \phi(x,t).
\]
The action becomes an integral over spacetime,
\[
S[\phi]=\int \dd t\int \dd x\,\mathcal L(\phi,\partial_t\phi,\partial_x\phi).
\]
For a simple string, the equation of motion takes the wave-equation form
\[
\partial_t^2\phi-v^2\partial_x^2\phi=0.
\]
The important conceptual step is that a field theory has a continuous set of dynamical variables, one for each point in space.


\section{Detailed derivation of the oscillator}

The action of the number operator on an eigenstate gives
\[
[N,a]|n\rangle
= N(a|n\rangle)-a(N|n\rangle)
= N(a|n\rangle)-n(a|n\rangle)
=-a|n\rangle.
\]
Therefore
\[
N(a|n\rangle)=(n-1)(a|n\rangle).
\]
The state $a|n\rangle$ is an eigenstate of $N$ with eigenvalue $n-1$:
\[
a|n\rangle=\alpha|n-1\rangle.
\]
The normalization is determined by
\[
|\alpha|^2
=\langle n-1|\alpha^*\alpha|n-1\rangle
=\langle n|a^\dagger a|n\rangle
=\langle n|N|n\rangle=n.
\]
Choosing $\alpha=\sqrt n$ gives
\[
a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle.
\]
For the vacuum state,
\[
a|0\rangle=0.
\]
The operator $a$ is therefore the annihilation operator: it removes one unit of $N$, i.e. one quantum of energy $\hbar\omega$. Conversely,
\[
a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle
\]
is the creation operator.

\section{From discrete variables to a field}

A field theory is a theory with a continuum of variables, rather than a set of discrete variables. Consider an elastic string modelled as an infinite chain of point masses attached by identical springs. The set
\[
\{\varphi_i\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\}
\]
describes a possible state of the discrete system.

For short-range interactions, the Hamiltonian is
\[
H=\sum_i\left[
\frac12m\dot\varphi_i^{\,2}
+\frac12 k(\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i)^2
\right].
\]
Factoring the lattice spacing $a$ gives
\[
H=a\sum_i\left[
\frac12\frac{m}{a}\dot\varphi_i^{\,2}
+\frac12ka\left(
\frac{\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i}{a}
\right)^2
\right].
\]
The corresponding Lagrangian is
\[
L=a\sum_i\left[
\frac12\frac{m}{a}\dot\varphi_i^{\,2}
-\frac12ka\left(
\frac{\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i}{a}
\right)^2
\right].
\]
The Euler--Lagrange equation is
\[
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\varphi_i}
-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\varphi_i}=0,
\]
which gives
\[
\frac{m}{a}\ddot\varphi_i
-ka\frac{\varphi_{i+1}-2\varphi_i+\varphi_{i-1}}{a^2}=0.
\]

\subsection{Continuum limit}

The continuum limit is taken as $a\to0$ while
\[
\frac{m}{a}\longrightarrow\mu,
\qquad
ka\longrightarrow\chi,
\qquad
ia\longrightarrow x.
\]
Here $\mu$ is the mass density, $\chi$ is Young's modulus, and $x$ is the position along the string. The discrete variable becomes the displacement field
\[
\varphi_i(t)\longrightarrow\varphi(x,t).
\]
A discrete set of variables becomes a continuous set of variables, assigning a physical quantity to every space-time point.

The difference is expanded as
\[
\varphi_{i+1}(t)-\varphi_i(t)
=\varphi(x+a,t)-\varphi(x,t)
=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}a+O(a^2).
\]
The sum becomes an integral:
\[
a\sum_i\longrightarrow\int_0^L\dd x.
\]
Thus
\[
S=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\dd t\int_0^L\dd x\,\mathcal L,
\]
with Lagrangian density
\[
\mathcal L
=\frac12\mu(\partial_t\varphi)^2
-\frac12\chi(\partial_x\varphi)^2.
\]
The notes write the continuum equation of motion as
\[
-\mu\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)^2
+\chi\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}\right)^2=0.
\]

In general,
\[
\mathcal L=\mathcal L(\partial_t\varphi,\partial_x\varphi;t).
\]
The action is local because it is an integral over time and space of a Lagrangian density depending on the value of the field and its derivatives at one point. The Lagrangian density is a polynomial. The Lagrangian formalism treats time and space on the same footing, as required by relativity. The Hamiltonian makes time explicit while space is disentangled.

\section*{Summary}
The interaction picture separates free evolution from interactions, while the harmonic oscillator shows how quantized excitations arise. The continuum limit replaces discrete coordinates by fields and sums by spatial integrals.
